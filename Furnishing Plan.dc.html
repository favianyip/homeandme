Making the model readable, then furnishing it

Build plan 1 August 2026 Four features, four phases

The plan in short

Build in the order the dependencies force, not the order of interest. Labels and the entrance door first — they cost little and make everything else legible. Balcony parapets second — small, visible, and it is the one change that opens a wall, so it needs its own guard. Furniture last, because a decorating system is only as good as the room types it is decorating, and room typing is the weakest link we have.

The one thing I would push back on: furnishing makes a broken model look finished. Every gate must keep running on the bare shell, and a "bare shell" toggle has to stay in the product, or we lose the ability to see what we are actually building.

1. Room labels in the 3D

The data already exists — every contract room carries a name, an area and a polygon, so this is presentation only. Two label surfaces, because they serve different jobs:

  • Floor lettering — the room name and area laid flat on the floor, aligned to the room's long axis. Right for the dollhouse view, for screenshots and for anything printed, and it survives export as real geometry.
  • Floating tags — a small billboard at eye height that turns to face the camera, for the walkthrough view where floor text is edge-on and unreadable.

Two rules that matter more than the typography. A room whose name came from the WC inference reads "BATH / WC — suggested", never a bare name; the label is the most likely place for a guess to launder itself into a fact. And an unnamed room reads "UNNAMED" with its area, rather than being left blank — a blank room looks like a rendering bug, whereas "UNNAMED · 6.1 m²" reads as a thing to fix and points at the dropdown that fixes it.

2. The entrance door

You are right that it is missing, and the cause is a classification rule rather than a detection failure. An opening with a room on only one side is currently called a window — so the front door, which by definition has the outside on one side, can never be a door. The panel duly reports "0 entrance".

The test for an entrance, in order: an opening on the outer boundary, 850–1200 mm wide, with no wall ink below it (a window sits on a cill — HDB requires cills of at least 1.0 m — while a door runs to the floor), giving onto a hall, foyer or living space. Where more than one candidate survives, prefer the facade carrying the fewest windows: that is the access corridor side, because corridor elevations are largely blank. Exactly one entrance per flat.

Rendered as a real leaf with its swing arc on the floor, in a distinct finish, tagged ENTRANCE, with the HDB metal gate as an option. It also repairs something structural: the walkability check currently starts from the largest room when it cannot find an entrance, so it has been proving "you can reach everything from the living room" rather than from the front door. This is a detection change, so it goes through the corpus with doors and clicks-to-fix measured before and after.

3. Balconies: a parapet, not a wall

Correct, and it is a real modelling error rather than a cosmetic one: the extruder gives every boundary the same 2.6 m, so a balcony comes out as a sealed box. What it should be is a low parapet with open air above and no ceiling.

HDB's own technical requirements give the numbers to build against. Parapets and railings at access corridors, lift lobbies and common areas must be at least 1.20 m above finished floor level (1.25 m from structural level, allowing 50 mm for screed). Window cills must be at least 1.0 m above internal finished floor. Gaps in a parapet or railing must not exceed 100 mm, the barrier must not be climbable — so no horizontal railings — and where a parapet is part solid, the solid portion must be at least 500 mm high. HDB also does not permit glass for parapets, which decides the default infill for us.

Note what those figures do and do not cover. The 1.20 m is written for common areas — corridors, lobbies, roof gardens. For a private balcony inside a flat, HDB's renovation guidance sets the parapet wall at not less than 1,000 mm above finished floor level, the same figure as a window cill. So we build private balcony parapets to 1.10 m by default — comfortably above the minimum rather than exactly on it — and expose the height as a tweak. For condominium plans a full-height glazed balustrade is the norm and the HDB glass restriction does not apply; that is a mode switch, not a different algorithm.

One correction fell out of reading those requirements, and is already applied rather than planned: the extruder had been building window cills at 0.9 m, 100 mm below the standard. Nobody would notice it in a walkthrough, but it mattered twice over — a stated requirement quietly missed, and the furniture solver's rule for what may stand in front of a window is written against the cill height. It now builds at 1.0 m, clamped to the wall height so the dollhouse cut does not leave glazing floating above the cut walls.

The guard on this feature matters as much as the feature. Opening a wall on a guess puts a hole in a walkthrough, so the parapet is only substituted when the room is confidently a balcony — named as one, or a perimeter space with no windows sitting outside the flat's enclosed line. Otherwise the wall stays full height and the room is flagged for review. Failing closed is the right default when the failure mode is a hole in a wall.

4. The automatic decorating system

This is the substantial piece, and it has one hard dependency worth stating before any of the design: furniture quality is bounded by room typing. A bed placed in a kitchen is a worse experience than an empty room, and right now some rooms carry no name at all while others carry a name that rode along with a merge. So the first component is not furniture.

4.1 Room typing

Infer a type for every unnamed room from evidence we already hold: floor area, minor dimension, fixture ink, position relative to the entrance, and adjacency. The largest space adjoining the entrance is living/dining; a 9–14 m² room off a hallway is a bedroom; a room carrying kitchen fixture ink next to the service side is the kitchen. Each inference carries a confidence, and only rooms above the confidence bar get furnished — the rest stay empty and say why, which is honest and also stops one bad guess from producing an absurd room.

4.2 The catalogue

Parametric modern pieces at HDB dimensions — no imported models, so everything stays light and exportable. Each piece declares its footprint, height, where it may sit, and the clearance it needs. A representative slice:

Table 1 — Dimensions are the ones that make a small flat work; they are the constraint, not a style choice. Finishes: light oak, off-white, charcoal, one brass accent — four materials for the whole catalogue.

4.3 The placement solver

Deterministic and rule-driven — the same instinct that fixed the geometry. No learned model, so every placement can be explained and any bad one traced to the rule that caused it.

  1. Occupancy grid at 100 mm over the room polygon; walls, columns and fixtures blocked.
  2. Reserve circulation before placing anything. A 900 mm route linking every door in the room, plus 600 mm clear in front of each door's swing. Furniture may never intrude — the walkthrough has to stay walkable with the furniture in it.
  3. Place anchors by rule — bed, sofa, dining table, kitchen run. These are the pieces with a correct answer; get them right and the rest follows.
  4. Fill companions greedily, with backtracking, scored on wall contact, alignment to the room's axes, clearance margin and distance to their anchor.
  5. Hard rejections: overlap, blocking a door swing, breaking circulation, crossing a room boundary, or standing in front of a window when the piece is taller than the 1.0 m cill. A piece that cannot be placed legally is not placed, and the room reports what it could not fit.

4.4 New gates

Furnishing earns its own checks, on the same principle as the geometry ones: walkable when furnished (the route survives with everything in place), no piece overlapping a wall, door swing or another piece, and every confident bedroom has a bed that fits. Critically, the existing watertight and reachability gates keep running on the bare shell. Furniture must never be able to mask a hole in a wall.

5. Sequence

Phase Work Exit criterion
1 · labels + entrance Floor lettering, floating tags, entrance classification and leaf Every room named or marked UNNAMED in 3D; an entrance found on at least 12 of the 14 corpus sheets with no loss of doors or clicks
2 · balcony Parapet substitution, no ceiling, floor finish, threshold, condo mode Balconies read as open on every sheet that has one, and no flat gains a hole anywhere else
3 · furnish core Room typing, catalogue, solver; living, dining and bedrooms only Furnished flats still walkable on all 14; zero overlaps; every confident bedroom has a bed
4 · furnish rest Kitchen run, sanitaryware, balcony set, staging polish, tweaks A 4-room flat furnishes end to end with no manual correction

Table 2 — Phases 1 and 2 are independent and could run together. Phase 3 depends on room typing and on nothing else. Nothing in this table is built yet; the only change already applied is the 1.0 m window cill described in §3, which was a correction rather than a feature.

6. What worries me, and what I need

  • Furniture flatters a broken model. A sofa and a rug make a leaking room look like a finished home. Bare-shell toggle stays, gates keep running on the shell, and the panel keeps reporting geometry faults even when the view is furnished.
  • Room typing is the real risk, not the furniture. Absurd placements will almost always be a typing error wearing a sofa. Hence the confidence bar and the visibly empty room.
  • Your two plans are still outstanding. The hollow-wall style branch and the envelope problem both remain unverified, and furnishing a model whose walls are fragmentary will look far worse than furnishing a clean one. If those sheets are representative of what customers upload, fixing them should come before phase 3.
  • Two decisions from you. Should furnishing be on by default or opt-in? And does condominium mode — glazed balustrades, different room mix — matter in this build, or is HDB the whole scope for now?
