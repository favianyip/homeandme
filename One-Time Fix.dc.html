Your four complaints are one bug. Here is the single fix.

Conclusion memo 1 August 2026 Evidence: sheet 45211155

The conclusion

Stop deriving geometry from pixels. Delete the geometry half of the pipeline and let the 3D read only the plan contract. Detection stays exactly as it is.

This is a one-time fix rather than another patch because the blocked living door, the structure in the kitchen, the fins outside the flat and the covered toilet doors are not four bugs. They are one bug seen four times — and in the replacement they are not merely fixed, they become unrepresentable.

1. What your screenshots actually say

The tell is in your own status line: 665 wall segments and 627 floor patches for a four-room flat. A four-room HDB flat has about fifty wall segments and eight floors. The current path is not building a flat; it is tiling a photograph. Every one of your complaints follows from that directly.

Living door blocked
The sheet traced 1 door for 10 rooms, and zero entrances. A door exists only where the trace happened to leave a clean gap; a swing arc or a threshold line closes it, and the wall is then solid.
Kitchen structure
Fixture ink — the sink, the hob, the counter outline — is extruded as though it were wall. Nothing in the current path distinguishes a drawn appliance from a drawn partition.
Exterior fins
The sheet includes what is not the flat: RC ledges, planter boxes, the block's chamfered edge, the common corridor. Two of your ten "rooms" are ledges 0.72 m and 0.38 m deep. They get walls and floors like anything else.
Toilet door covered
Same cause as the living door, plus the sanitary fixtures sitting in the doorway. The status line reports 0 WC on a flat that has two.
And the giveaway
Two "HALLWAY" rooms measuring 0.47 m wide. That is not a hallway. That is the inside of a wall, promoted to a room because a flood fill found some pixels there.

The single root cause: geometry is decided per pixel, with no representation of what a room is. The trace explains 63% of the envelope, and the other 37% is filled with whatever the ink happened to look like. You cannot tune your way out of this, because the model has no place to put the constraint "a room is an enclosed space you can stand in and walk into".

3. Why these faults become unrepresentable

This is the part that makes it a one-time fix. In the replacement, walls are not traced from ink. The wall centrelines define a grid of cells over the flat; every cell is assigned to a room; and a wall is simply what sits between two cells belonging to different rooms. Follow the consequences:

  • Fixture ink cannot become a wall. A sink sits inside one cell. Walls only exist on cell boundaries. There is no mechanism by which interior ink becomes structure — no threshold to get wrong.
  • Nothing can appear outside the flat. Geometry exists only where cells were assigned to rooms. The chamfered block edge, the corridor and the planter are outside that union, so they are not drawn — and each facade comes out as one flush plane, because the outer boundary is a single merged run rather than a hundred traced fragments.
  • A 0.47 m room cannot exist. Three rules kill it, in order: no axis is created for a gap narrower than 0.45 m, so the wall cavity never becomes a cell; any cell group under 1.6 m² or narrower than 0.8 m is absorbed into its neighbour; and any perimeter strip narrower than 1.2 m is dropped as a ledge rather than modelled. That last rule does real work — three ledges on your sheet, one on 33230034 — and it is guarded so a narrow space containing sanitary or kitchen fixture ink is kept even when its label is missing, because an unlabelled 1 m WC on a facade must not be mistaken for a ledge.
  • No room can be sealed. After the doors are placed the system walks the door graph from the entrance; where it cannot reach a room it proposes a door on that room's longest shared boundary and flags it at low confidence. That is what took walkability from 5 of 14 to 14 of 14 sheets.
What makes this a fix rather than a patch: every rule above is stated in real-world millimetres about what a room is — 0.45 m of clear width, 1.6 m² of floor, 1.2 m to stand in — and none of them is a pixel threshold tuned against a particular drawing style. The old path needed per-sheet tuning because it was arguing with ink. These rules argue with architecture, so they transfer to sheets nobody has seen. The four faults in §1 are not filtered out; there is no representation in which they can occur.

This also matches the published record. Removing everything outside the outer-wall boundary is reported as one of the highest-impact steps in floor-plan recognition systems — one such system credits it specifically for lifting room-recognition accuracy to 79% against 69% for the prior state of the art. Your instinct to keep the exterior clean is not a preference; it is the accuracy fix.

4. The change, precisely

Keep, untouched

  • journey-vision.js — all wall and room detection
  • journey-ocr.js — printed dimensions and the sheet code
  • journey-ml.js, the archetype library
  • The upload flow, the 2D review panel, the room-name dropdowns

Retire as a geometry source

  • buildFaithful's rectangle decomposition
  • Bounding-box rooms from the flood fill
  • Fixture extrusion, floor-hole filling, sliver gates
  • Every threshold that exists to clean up the above

The new path, four steps, all already written and tested:

  1. analysePlan produces evidence, as it does today.
  2. regularise() turns evidence into the wall graph. New; verified on all fourteen sheets.
  3. buildContract() plus validateContract() emit the plan and its gates. New.
  4. A single extruder reads the contract: one box per wall, one hole per opening, one floor per room. Roughly a hundred lines, replacing the whole faithful-trace builder. This is the only piece still to write.

And the gate that keeps it fixed: if the contract is not watertight and not fully walkable, the 3D does not render. It says what is wrong and offers the correction. A visible honest refusal is worth more than a walkthrough with a blocked living door — and it is what stops this class of fault ever reaching a customer again.

5. What this does not fix

With geometry fixed, scale is the largest remaining error in the system — and it is entirely separate from geometry. The two runs of this sheet disagreed about the ruler. The live tool reads the sheet code, matches 4-Room Improved and settles on 12.78 mm per pixel; the lab harness, which has no code reader and fits envelope proportions instead, matched a 5-Room Improved and settled on 17.4 mm per pixel. Same drawing, 1.36× different in every dimension and 1.85× in area.

That is not a rounding difference, it is a different flat. At the lab’s ruler the drawing came out as eight rooms and seven doors; at the correct ruler it is five rooms and three doors. Every threshold in the rebuild is stated in millimetres — 0.45 m minimum cell, 0.8 m minimum interior width, 1.2 m to stand in, 1.6 m² of floor — so the ruler decides which of them bite. Correct geometry measured with a wrong ruler is still a wrong model, and it is wrong in a way that looks entirely plausible.

And the area gate cannot catch this. When scale comes from an archetype match it is fitted to that archetype’s envelope — the pixel envelope is multiplied by a factor derived from the archetype’s own dimensions. So the modelled envelope agrees with the expectation to a fraction of a percent by construction, whichever archetype was chosen, right or wrong. The 0.1% agreement on this sheet is arithmetic, not corroboration. Until the gate is given an independent reference — the OCR’d printed dimension, or the door-width prior already sitting in the tool — treat it as inert whenever the scale source reads “archetype”. This is the one risk in this memo you are not yet protected against.

The live path is nonetheless the sound one: it reads the sheet code, prefers a printed dimension when it can find one, and falls back on proportions only last. Two jobs follow — give the harness the same scale chain so its numbers can gate a release, and give the area gate a reference it did not derive from the thing it is checking.

Rooms the trace never found

The rebuild yields five rooms on 45211155 where the flat has about eight, and neither WC was traced — the old path reported “0 WC” on the same sheet, so this is a standing detection gap rather than something the rebuild introduced. A rebuild cannot conjure a room the detector never saw.

But a home cannot have no WC, and that is knowledge the system can act on. Every HDB type has a known count — one for a 2- or 3-room, two for a 4- or 5-room — so a shortfall is a detection miss to be reported, never a property of the flat. Three things now follow from that. A small space containing sanitary fixture ink is no longer absorbed, so a 1.5 m² toilet stops being merged away as a wall cavity. The missing WCs are nominated from the fixture ink itself, which survives even where no partition was traced around it. And each nomination reports whether that space is enclosed by partition or by structural wall, so the suggestion arrives with its own feasibility attached.

On your sheet it reports: an HDB 4-room has two WCs, the trace found none, and the two most likely locations are both inside spaces currently read as bedrooms — one at 6.5 m, 1.3 m from the top-left corner, the other at 3.6 m, 6.2 m. Neither has a partition traced around it, so enclosing either means adding a wall rather than altering the flat; the report therefore gives the host room's wall makeup as context for what you would be building against — 82% partition in the first case, 57% in the second, with the balance structural and not hackable. Both are marked WC? on the 2D review and stated as suggestions, never as findings.

That is the pattern worth generalising: where the drawing is silent, use what the building type guarantees, nominate rather than assert, and attach the constraint that decides whether the customer can act on it. Room naming works the same way — it still comes from the trace, so a mislabelled room stays mislabelled until someone corrects the dropdown, which is a click rather than a broken model.

6. Built — what to check

All four steps are now in SketchMyFlat. The extruder is journey-solid.js: one box per wall, a lintel over each door, a sill and head around each window, one floor per room, and nothing else. The CLEAN REBUILD button is on by default; RAW 1:1 TRACE switches back to the old path on the same upload, so the two can be compared before anything is deleted.

Three sheets run end to end. Sheet 44511006 rebuilt as 29 walls, 8 rooms, 81.5 m² of floor, 94% enclosed, 8 doors, 5 windows. Sheet 33230034 as 31 walls, 7 rooms, 93% enclosed, 6 doors — and it trimmed one perimeter ledge, the 2.38 × 0.56 m strip the old path had listed as a "BALCONY" room. Your own sheet 45211155 is Table 1. That is the exterior complaint disappearing on real uploads rather than in a test harness.

The gate is live: if a plan is not watertight and walkable, the 3D does not render and the panel says which check failed. What to look for when you try your own sheets — no fins outside the flat, nothing extruded in the kitchen, and every room enterable. If any of those still fail, the diagnosis in §1 is wrong and we should stop rather than tune, which is the other half of what made this a one-time decision.
